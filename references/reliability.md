# Reliability: will it run as scheduled?

Read this when an option has a change, a known punctuality problem, a `when_full` or
boat leg, or a likely disruption (strike, track works, weather, season), or when the
user asks about delays. Always read it for boats and ferries: check the day's gale
warning and the authority that suspends trips (for example the Philippine Coast Guard).

Reliability is separate from confidence. Confidence asks whether the information is
correct; reliability asks whether the service will actually run as published. A Deutsche
Bahn long-distance timetable can be Confirmed and still often late.

## The one-line note and realistic duration

Every option card gets `reliability`, one line, and a `realistic_duration` where
sources report one:

- from official punctuality figures where the operator publishes them (note how the
  operator counts: DB counts a train as on time if it is less than 6 minutes late and
  leaves cancelled trains out of the figures);
- otherwise from first-hand reports: "often 15–30 min late on this line", "leaves when
  full, can take hours", "usually on time", "winter trips often take 4–5 h instead of 3".

## Connection buffers

- Flag changes under about 15 minutes, and any change before the last service of the
  day, a border or a once-daily shuttle. Suggest an earlier departure or a bigger gap.
- Note whether one ticket covers the whole trip. With one ticket, missed connections are
  usually covered (BDZ, for example, holds connecting trains for through tickets);
  separate tickets often are not.

## Disruptions and where to check them

- **Strikes:** German rail unions usually announce strikes shortly beforehand; Italy
  publishes an official strike calendar in advance.
- **Track works with replacement buses** (Schienenersatzverkehr in Germany): the bus
  often leaves from a different spot than the train, so update the boarding point.
- **Weather and roads:** use the official road authority as the main source for whether
  a road is passable, and learn its wording. On the Georgian Military Road
  (georoad.gov.ge), a ban on trucks and trailers still lets cars through; a full closure
  does not, and nothing runs while it is posted. Ferries are cancelled in strong wind
  (in the Philippines the Coast Guard suspends trips); monsoon floods close roads.
- **Seasons:** summer-only services, shorter off-season timetables, earlier last
  departures in winter. Summer timetables dominate search results, so search the season
  and local month names.
- **Holidays:** locals may get priority (boats at Christmas), and services thin out.

## Tickets that protect you

- One ticket for the whole trip versus separate tickets (see above).
- EU rail passengers can claim part of the fare back for long delays.
- At DB, a ticket valid only on a specific train becomes valid on other trains once a
  delay of 20 minutes or more is expected.

## Official live status

Give the operator's live-status app or page so the user can check on the day, for
example DB Navigator in Germany, NTES in India, the BDZ journey planner in Bulgaria, or
the road authority's restrictions page for mountain passes.
